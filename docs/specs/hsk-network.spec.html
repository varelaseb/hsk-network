HSK network

docs / specs / hsk-network.spec.html

A public static website: a force-directed graph of the HSK 1 and HSK 2 vocabulary in Traditional characters.

Words that share a character cluster around that character; each word shows its Zhuyin and English definitions on hover or tap.

User stories

As a learner, I see every HSK 1 and HSK 2 word as a network in which words sharing a character gather around that character, so I learn characters through related words.

As a learner, I hover or tap a word to see its Traditional form, Zhuyin, English definitions, and HSK level, and I tap a character hub to see its Zhuyin, its English, and the words that contain it, each with a short English gloss.

As a learner, I see in a character hub's card its radical and the parts it is built from, each with a short English meaning and, where the part is a character itself, its Zhuyin, so I learn to read characters by their parts.

As a learner, I see a hub's character drawn in simple lines in its card with each part already in its own color and the radical marked, and a matching swatch beside each entry in the Radical and Parts rows, so I see where each part sits at a glance without tapping anything.

As a learner, I type into a search box to find a word and the graph brings it into view.

As a learner, I switch each HSK level on or off so I can study one level's words at a time.

As a learner on a phone, I can read, pan, zoom, and tap the graph as comfortably as on a desktop.

As a learner, I open a link to one word, such as from the bubbles game, and the network shows that word focused with its card open.

As a learner, I go from the network to the bubbles game in one tap.

As a learner, I can see where the vocabulary, definitions, and pronunciation recordings come from and under which licenses.

As a learner, I tap a speaker button on a word card or on a reading in a character card to hear it said, and I tap a character, a syllable, or a single Zhuyin symbol in a card to hear just that sound, so I learn to say what I read.

As a learner, I turn pronunciation on or off once with one Voice control, and the network and the game both follow it.

As a learner, I read a word's meanings one per line in plain English, with its measure words labeled, and every Chinese form in Traditional characters with Zhuyin, so nothing on a card looks like dictionary code.

As a learner, I pick once whether the site and the game show Traditional characters with Zhuyin or Simplified characters with pinyin, and every word, card, label, bubble, and search follows that choice.

As a learner, the site and the game look calm and polished, read clearly in one typeface family, and move smoothly on a phone, without motion when my device asks for less.

As a learner whose device uses the dark appearance, I see the network page and every card in one calm night look, so nothing glares white at night.

As a learner, I can see where the radicals, parts, part meanings, and character drawings come from and under which terms.

As a maintainer, I rebuild the word data with one command, and an automated test suite proves the data correct on every pull request.

Modular boundaries

The data pipeline owns turning the HSK lists, CC-CEDICT, the pronunciation recordings, Unihan, and the IDS data into the graph data, and GlyphWiki into the drawing data: matching, Traditional forms, Zhuyin, definitions cleaned into readable senses and measure words, hubs, links, which word has a recording, which syllables have a recording, each hub's radical and parts, and each hub's strokes with which strokes belong to each part and to the radical. Caller-facing seam: one build command that writes the graph data file, the drawing data file, and the word and syllable recordings, plus both files' schemas below. Dependency direction: external provider; it downloads CC-CEDICT, the recordings, Unihan, the IDS data, and the GlyphWiki data at build time only, and nothing calls back into it. Observable scope: the committed graph data file, the committed drawing data file, the committed word and syllable recordings, and the build's error list.

The site owns layout, rendering, hover and tap cards including the hub breakdown and its drawn character, search, legend, word links, the Play link, the Voice control, speaker buttons and tappable readings in cards, the Sources footer, and the shared look in both appearances: typefaces, type scale, color, and motion, which the game page reuses. Caller-facing seam: the public page and its word link address. Dependency direction: self-contained; it consumes the graph data file, the drawing data file, and the vendored graph library only, and holds no dictionary or stroke logic: it draws the strokes it is given, colors each part's strokes, and outlines the radical's. Observable scope: what a learner sees and touches.

One shared voice module, used by the network page and the game, owns the Voice setting and its storage, the Zhuyin symbol name table, choosing a sound's source by the sound rules below, and playing one sound at a time. Caller-facing seam: a page asks whether a sound can be said, to show or hide its speaker button, and asks it to say a word, a reading, a syllable, or a symbol; it reads and changes the Voice setting. Dependency direction: self-contained; both pages depend on it; it reads the graph data the page already loaded and the device's voice list, and knows nothing of cards, bubbles, or the pipeline. Observable scope: what a learner hears, the Voice control's state on both pages, and the unit tests of the choice and symbol table.

The font build owns cutting the two vendored typefaces down to the characters the site and game show, as three files: Geist, the Chinese face, and its mainland forms face. Caller-facing seam: one maintainer command that reads the graph data file and the pages' own text and writes the three subset font files and their coverage list. Dependency direction: external provider; it uses the fontTools subsetter at maintainer time only, and the site and tests never call it. Observable scope: the committed font files, their coverage list, and the font size budget.

Delivery owns tests on every pull request and publishing the site on every merge to main. Caller-facing seam: the pull request test check and the public address. Dependency direction: external provider GitHub Actions and GitHub Pages; it runs the repository's own test command and publishes the site directory unchanged. Observable scope: check status and the live site.

Graph model

Word nodes link only to character hubs.

Each HSK list entry is one word node, so a character with two listed readings, such as 長 cháng and 長 zhǎng, is two word nodes told apart by their Zhuyin.

A character hub exists for every Traditional character that appears in two or more word nodes, counting a single-character word as containing its character.

Each word links once to the hub of each distinct character it contains; a word whose characters are all unshared stays unlinked and floats near the center.

Word nodes are larger than hubs; hubs are gray and labeled with their character in a smaller size.

Matching an entry to CC-CEDICT

  1. Candidates are CC-CEDICT entries whose Simplified form equals the list entry's and whose pinyin equals it after both are reduced to lowercase syllables with tone numbers, treating a missing tone as neutral (5) and ü as u:.
  2. When both a capitalized (proper noun) and a lowercase candidate match, keep those whose capitalization matches the list entry.
  3. Remaining candidates sharing one Traditional form merge their definitions in dictionary order, de-duplicated.
  4. Definitions that only point elsewhere, those starting "variant of", "old variant of", "see ", or "surname ", are dropped unless nothing else remains.
  5. No candidate, or candidates with differing Traditional forms, fails the build and names each such entry.
  6. A small committed override file resolves each failing entry by naming the CC-CEDICT Traditional form and pinyin to use, with a one-line reason per override.
  7. A list word absent from CC-CEDICT is overridden with its Traditional form, pinyin, and hand-written English definitions, and its reason says the definitions are not from CC-CEDICT.

Character readings

Each per-character fact lives once, in the character table of the graph data file keyed by character: a character's readings and its short meaning are stored there and nowhere else, and hubs, cards, radicals, and parts refer to a character only by the character itself.

A one-character word stores no Zhuyin or definitions of its own: it records which reading of its character's table entry the HSK list means, the reading whose pinyin equals the word's matched pinyin, and the site and game read its Zhuyin and definitions through that reading; a one-character word whose reading is missing from its character's entry fails the build and names it. A word of two or more characters keeps its own matched Zhuyin and definitions.

A character's readings, stored in its character table entry for every hub character and every radical or part that is a character itself, are the lowercase single-character CC-CEDICT entries whose Traditional form is that character, or, for a radical or part that is a character itself only by its Simplified form, whose Simplified form is, one reading per distinct pinyin, the reading matching the first value of the character's Unihan kMandarin first and the rest in dictionary order, so the standard reading shows first, such as 舌 ㄕㄜˊ before ㄐㄧ, each with its Zhuyin and its definitions merged and dropped by the match rules above; capitalized entries count only when no lowercase entry exists, and a character with no entry at all fails the build and names it.

Every character in any word's Traditional form has its own entry with readings in the graph data file's character table, by the character readings rule, whether or not it is a hub; the game's character card reads it there.

Character breakdown

  1. A hub's radical is the Kangxi radical named by the first kRSUnicode value of its character, recorded on the hub as that radical's character from the CJK radicals table and its radical number.
  2. A hub's parts are the top-level components of its character's IDS sequence tagged T (Taiwan), or its first sequence when none is tagged T; repeated parts are listed once, in sequence order.
  3. A character whose IDS sequence is the character itself has no parts.
  4. Every radical and part has a character table entry holding its meaning: the first semicolon clause of the Unihan kDefinition that does not name a radical, without parenthetical text, cut at its first comma.
  5. A radical or part is a character itself when it has a lowercase single-character CC-CEDICT entry, by Traditional or Simplified form, with a definition that does not mention "radical"; its character table entry then also holds its readings by the character readings rule below, and the card shows the Zhuyin of the first; otherwise the entry has no readings and the card shows no Zhuyin.
  6. A part or radical with no meaning, such as a stroke, an unencoded component, or a rare form with no kDefinition, fails the build and names the hub character.
  7. The committed override file also takes one entry per such character, naming its parts and each part's meaning, or no parts, with a one-line reason; an override entry may also replace a misleading derived meaning, with its reason.

Character drawing

  1. A hub's glyph is the GlyphWiki glyph named for its code point with the Taiwan suffix, such as u8a71-t for 話, or the plain code point name when no Taiwan glyph exists; a glyph that only points to another glyph is followed to it.
  2. Its strokes are the glyph's skeleton strokes in data order, each component the glyph refers to placed in its box and expanded in turn; each stroke is kept as a centerline path on the 200-unit GlyphWiki grid.
  3. A part's strokes are those of every top-level component whose code point is that part, so a part used twice takes both; when exactly one part is left unnamed, every remaining stroke is its.
  4. The radical's strokes are those of the part that is the radical, by its own Kangxi radical number with no extra strokes, so 訁 counts for 言; every stroke when the hub character is the radical itself.
  5. The committed override file's entry for a hub may name, by stroke position, the strokes of each part and of the radical, with a one-line reason; it replaces the rules above for that hub.
  6. A hub with no glyph, with two or more parts left unnamed, with a part or radical naming no stroke, or with an override whose positions overlap, leave a stroke out, or fall outside the drawing fails the build and names the character.

The drawing data is its own file in the site directory, beside the graph data and never merged into it, because each data kind has one file and the two carry different licenses; it holds per hub only strokes and stroke positions, and a hub's readings, meanings, and parts are read from the graph data as before.

Measure words and cross-references share one reference shape, Traditional, Simplified, pinyin, and Zhuyin; it records the dictionary's citation, the reading the entry names, and is not a character fact, so it sits beside the sense rather than in the character table.

After cleaning, a sense whose plain text, outside its references, still holds a character CC-CEDICT knows only as a Simplified form, a tone-numbered syllable, a vertical bar, or a square bracket fails the build and names the entry, so a new CC-CEDICT pattern is caught before it ships.

One small sense-rendering module, shared by the network page and the game, turns a sense and its references into what the current script mode shows, and owns turning tone-numbered pinyin into tone marks.

The rules apply to word definitions and to character readings alike, so word cards, hub cards, search results, and every game card show the same cleaned text.

Matching a word to a recording

  • A word gets the recording whose name is its Simplified form; a word with no such recording gets none and the build lists it, without failing.
  • When two list entries share a Simplified form, such as 長 cháng and 長 zhǎng, neither gets a recording, because one clip cannot say both readings.
  • A word with a recording carries its site-relative path in the graph data file; one without carries no audio entry.

Matching a syllable to a recording

  • A syllable is named by its lowercase numbered pinyin with ü written v, such as xue2, lv4, or men5; its recording is the syllable set's file of that name.
  • The needed syllables are every syllable of every word, every reading in the character table, every measure word and reference, and the name syllable of every Zhuyin symbol.
  • An erhua ㄦ written as its own syllable is said as the symbol ㄦ, so it needs no syllable of its own.
  • A needed syllable in tones 1 to 4 with no recording in the set and no override fails the build and names it; a neutral-tone syllable gets a recording only when the set has one, and the build lists the rest without failing.
  • The committed override file resolves each such syllable with a one-line reason, either by naming the set's file that records it under another spelling, such as ju4 recorded as jv4, or by stating that the set has none, such as bia1; a syllable stated as having none has no recording and is said by the device voice.
  • The graph data file lists the syllables that have a recording, and each is copied to the site's syllable folder under its name; no other syllable file is in the site.

Every text style names Geist first and Noto Sans CJK TC second, so Latin text takes Geist and every character, Zhuyin symbol, and tone mark falls through to the Chinese face; Zhuyin never renders in the Latin face.

The Chinese font is cut down by the font build to exactly the Traditional and Simplified characters in the graph data file and the pages' own text, plus all 37 Zhuyin symbols, the four tone marks, and CJK punctuation, in their Taiwan shapes only; the mainland forms face is cut from the same release to only those characters shown in pinyin mode whose mainland shape differs, each mapped straight to its mainland glyph; Geist is cut to Basic Latin, Latin-1, the pinyin tone letters of Latin Extended, combining tone marks, and typographic punctuation.

Chinese text is tagged zh-Hant in Zhuyin mode and zh-Hans in pinyin mode, for voices and assistive technology; the glyph shape comes from the face, never the tag, so it holds where a browser ignores the tag, as Safari does for text drawn on a canvas.

In pinyin mode every text style and every bubble names the mainland forms face before the Chinese face, so a character with a mainland shape takes it and every other falls through; Zhuyin mode never names it, so it is fetched only once pinyin mode is used.

Size budget, as WOFF2: the Chinese subset and the mainland forms face together at most 232 KB, Geist at most 23 KB, both at most 255 KB; each font is subset to the characters used and served from the site, with no runtime third-party fetch; both are preloaded and swap in when ready, and the game draws its bubbles only once the Chinese faces its script setting names have loaded.

The font build writes a coverage list beside the fonts naming every character in each Chinese file and each file's hash, because the standard library cannot read WOFF2; the test suite checks coverage and hash from that list.

A character the Chinese face lacks, such as the rare hub part 𠬛, is named in the coverage list and falls back to the device font.

Zhuyin layout

A headword pairs its characters with the space-separated syllables of its Zhuyin in order; each character is followed by a column of its syllable's symbols, the tone mark for tones 2, 3, and 4 to the right of the last symbol, and the neutral-tone dot centered above the first.

In pinyin mode a headword shows each character's pinyin syllable, with its tone mark, centered above the character; lists, measure words, and references show the pinyin on one line after the characters, syllables of one word joined without spaces.

Pinyin renders in Geist; a tone letter Geist has no precomposed glyph for, ǐ, ǒ, ǔ, and ü with any tone, renders as its base letter followed by a combining tone mark.

When a headword's syllable count differs from its character count, its Zhuyin runs on one line under it instead.

On one line, a tone mark sits directly after its syllable with no space, the neutral-tone dot directly before it, and syllables are separated by a gap of 0.3em.
